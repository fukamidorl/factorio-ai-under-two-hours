# Development history

[Human README](../../README.md) · [AI operations](AI_OPERATIONS.md) · [日本語](../ja/DEVELOPMENT_HISTORY.md) · [简体中文](../zh-Hans/DEVELOPMENT_HISTORY.md)

These trials took place in November 2026. Early runs used Factorio 2.1.24. The mass-production runs and packaged route used the 2.1.25 base game. Automation times below use the in-game clock from automation startup unless specified.

## Through November 22 · From one iron plate to the first rocket

The user wanted AI or a program to launch a rocket from a fresh map on their installed game. The first test connected Factorio LLM Use 0.1.1 through `uv`, smelted one iron plate in a fresh 2.1.24 test world and collected it. This gave the project a small test of the connection and controls. [Factorio LLM Use](https://github.com/Tsyklop/factorio-llm-use)

The user asked for a world that kept enemies at a distance and could support an achievement run. The initial seed, `210539101`, had the raw materials on a starting landmass isolated by water. The plan used steam power and needed no uranium. Working backward from the launch produced a materials list, a research schedule and blueprints. A staged trial carried the same save through to a satellite launch and victory at **5:49:24**. A separate result at **3:59:38** marked silo research and readiness of the three rocket-part ingredients. Copper, steel and circuits were shared among purple science, yellow science and rocket production, which left some lines short of input.

The Steam personal achievements page showed Steam power at 17:31, No time for chitchat, Raining bullets, Steam all the way, and Crafting with productivity at 18:50, and There is no spoon at 21:02 on November 22 (Japan time). The 21:02 unlock matches the trial that resumed the earlier seed's Lua-assisted save just before victory in the 2.1.24 base game with the custom mod disabled. The in-game notification appeared after victory at 5:49:24. [Steam achievement verification](../../reference/development_evidence.json)

## November 22–23 · Use human speedruns to revise the route

The user treated two hours as a target and asked us to learn from the trials by comparing the routes and the design of finished factories. We examined sustained output from the mines through smelting, research and the next construction stage. The source list also includes the full-run comparison that took place after the seed change on November 23.

## November 23 · Change the seed, compare full runs and test the game display

The user proposed a broad seed search and testing high-throughput layouts in the game. We compared 1,279 combinations drawn from 288 new seeds and several map settings. The chosen coastal setting for **4263722700** had stone frequency at 150%, copper 23 tiles away, coal 18 tiles away and a 128×128 expansion area. Enemy generation stayed enabled. The surveyed starting landmass was enclosed by water and had no nests or worms. The oilfield was roughly 236 tiles away and needed piping. [Packaged map settings](../../world/map-gen-settings.json)

The first 45-minute test ended with seven powered electric miners and 15 green packs consumed. The selected comparison used southeast copper and started with two red-science assemblers, added electric coal and iron, then expanded red science to four assemblers. It reached **49 powered miners and 218 green packs consumed**. This favored commissioning smaller rows early with power, input and output already in place.

At the user's request, we played the whole Zaspar Default Settings recording and matched sampled events with the production statistics in the published save. **1:59:01** is the registered real time and the victory screen showed **1:58:32** of game time. Since the overlay timer was reset during the recording, video position and the save clock anchored the comparisons. The analysis measured tasks, equipment and output. Its input-speed evidence was limited to visible activity. Map settings, enemy rules, payload, clocks and the older rocket control unit recipe affected the comparison. [Default Settings victory screen](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2PwyPjjoOJo&t=7153s)

At game time 0:20–0:30, the human run produced around **1,606 iron plates per minute** and our early run produced about **193**. From 1:42 to 1:52 the runner produced roughly 67 blue, 101 purple and 105 yellow packs per minute. Our trial at that point had yet to make purple or yellow packs. We began tracking building materials, actual operating equipment, distribution and the timing of expansions together. [Extracted comparison data](../../reference/development_evidence.json)

In the public save, belts fed 16 of the 56 main labs, with inserters passing packs between labs. The silo had four productivity module 3s and 20 surrounding beacons. Our integrated run supplied labs through Lua and installed four speed module 3s in the silo. We applied the lessons about parallel work and continuous supply to the equipment that suited our route. [Runner’s main factory](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2PwyPjjoOJo&t=6428s) · [Runner’s silo before launch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2PwyPjjoOJo&t=7137s)

When the user asked what else could happen while research ran, we found four gaps without new construction orders in the 137-minute test, totaling **55:44**. Some were deliberate measurement windows and production and research kept running. We turned minute 45, minute 96 and other fixed triggers into observation points. Oil, steel and steam construction could advance once technology, parts and power were ready. In a steel-district test, 24 robots built the same 584 entities in 6:19, compared with 12:02 using 12 robots.

The user also wanted to see the game. In the Windows input test, sustained movement and mining inputs allowed the engineer to collect one iron plate on screen. A separate display mod reproduced that small task. For the rocket run we chose a real Factorio window with Lua driving gameplay.

## November 24 · Run the scaled factory end to end

The user asked for production, supply and power sized against human speedruns, with enough material upstream to keep later stages working. Scaling brought the checkpointed run from **7:27:37 to 6:56:53**. Revisions were loaded into the same world during the run. Early iron output was 75 plates/min while gears accumulated. Power expansion sent water into an unfinished pipe, and crash debris blocked a pole and a collection point. Yellow science ran short of processing units, and the silo ran short of electric engine units. The timing of usable capacity and distribution remained the main constraints.

The next run prioritized the first six coal, six iron and three copper drills, power connections and steam parts. It upgraded furnaces to steel, supplemented coal with excess solid fuel, and expanded refining and cracking with demand. The silo, satellite and landing pad were prepared during research. It launched at **2:13:44**. The first blue packs appeared at 0:54 and silo research began at 1:56. Purple supply fell to 17 packs/min over 1:45–1:55. Stone bricks, electric furnaces, petroleum gas and plastic shortages became the next targets. [Events before and during the supply fixes](../../reference/development_evidence.json)

## November 24 · Combine the sub-two-hour changes and repeat the run

| Bottleneck | Change and observed result |
| --- | --- |
| Missing parts for the power expansion | Reserve iron for the steam engines and intermediate parts such as pipes. Prioritize power equipment while capacity is low, and use both coal and surplus solid fuel. The integrated route reached about 130.5 MW of observed generation and 163.8 MW of final rated capacity. |
| Red and green supply and research order | Expand dedicated red/green production and delivery. Drop construction robotics and mining productivity 1 and 2 from pre-launch research. That cut the prerequisite totals by 850 red packs, 850 green packs and 600 blue packs. The earlier robot experiments still informed the order that brought finished production blocks online. |
| Blue science, steel and purple science competing for inputs | Prioritize engines and pipes while blue supply is thin. Lower the rail stock cap from 4,800 to 1,800 and upgrade steel furnaces earlier. Allocate raw stone according to brick and rail stocks, and fit three manufactured speed module 1s into each of 20 stone drills. In the integrated run, stone increased from 615 to 943/min and purple recovered from 47/min at 1:35–1:40 to 100/min at 1:40–1:45. |
| Oil, yellow science and launch parts competing for inputs | Expand advanced oil processing and both types of cracking as feedstock allows. Cap the yellow science allocation based on remaining research plus a buffer, releasing processing units and low density structures for the rocket. Periods of thin petroleum gas and plastic stocks remained. |
| Construction around the silo and slow assembly | Reuse three assemblers freed by completed research to get around crash debris and pole conflicts. Fit four speed module 3s in the silo. Placement to launch dropped from 5:35 to about 2:15. |

A checkpointed trial with successive revisions launched at **1:50:27**. The first fresh run with every change present from the start took **2:02:08**. A site whose pole could not connect blocked construction, speed module tiers 2 and 3 arrived too late, and purple science lacked module inputs. We skipped sites that could not connect and prioritized productivity module 1 inputs for purple and speed module 2 and 3 inputs for the silo.

With the fixed code used from a fresh start, the integrated run launched at **1:49:38** and received cargo at **1:49:47**. Both passes switched the same save to a local server at minute 65. All 173 recorded event ticks matched. The first blue packs arrived six minutes later than in the 2:13:44 run. Purple arrived at 1:16, yellow at 1:20 and silo research started at 1:31:50, making the final launch 24:06 earlier. This shows why we tracked the finish and later supply alongside each local milestone. [Integrated events and save inspection](../../reference/confirmed_runs.json)

## November 24 · Visible demonstration, package validation and precedents

The earlier visible run launched at **1:52:59**, and the user saw the victory screen. Its **1:53:21** playtime included the intro. The earlier package used a save from before player creation. Verification followed the headless run through launch and delivery and the visible run through the camera switch after steam power. Headless results use launch and delivery. Visible results use launch and victory.

For public distribution, the launcher was changed to generate the starting world locally in Factorio 2.1.25. The headless run launched at **1:50:18** and received 1,000 space science packs at **1:50:27**. All 172 recorded events matched the earlier repeat. [Package verification](../../reference/package_verification.json)
